A family at the cove

This resort trip was yours before it was ours. You had planned it for your family before my own visit was even arranged. I loved that about it. I wasn't asking your family to revolve around me; I was fortunate enough to be included in something you already wanted to give them.

Even before we left, the resort outing had a meaning I didn't want to miss. You'd planned it for your family before my own trip was arranged, and I had been welcomed into those plans. We packed groceries and overnight bags into a large van. With everyone aboard, you and I had our own spot near the front, and for a while the drive was its own entertainment. Little Zia's “Jingle Bells” performance is the kind of family moment no elaborate attraction could have improved. She seemed almost cautious with the verses, then burst into the chorus as if she'd been saving all her energy for it. I can still hear the laughter.

Reaching Forest Cove took more effort than pulling up to a hotel entrance. We needed to find the right place to board a boat, asked around for directions, and took a narrow road that didn't inspire much confidence. Then came the boat ride itself. All those practical steps mattered because the reward was shared: family gathered for a night by the water, eating together, swimming, singing, taking pictures, and enjoying something you had wanted to give them.

I watched you as much as I watched the sea. The way you cared for everyone, the way you made sure things worked, the way Benj could relax and play around you—it all deepened what I'd already begun to feel. The black dress you wore that day remains vivid in my mind. So does the moment I carried Benj on my shoulders. We weren't suddenly an established family, and I wouldn't pretend we were. But I remember looking at you and him together, happy in the water, and thinking how grateful I was that I'd gotten on the plane. I wasn't just having a great trip. I was seeing a life I wanted to belong to.

We packed up and waited for the big passenger van. You took pictures of us while we waited. Once everyone was aboard, you and I sat together on the first bench behind the front seats. The drive became its own little story, especially when little Zia started singing “Jingle Bells” with Tita Tinay. She would sing the verses quietly, almost cautiously, but when the chorus came she couldn't contain herself. She belted it out, sometimes a beat early, and everyone laughed. I can still picture the excitement.

Reaching Forest Cove Beach House at Natipuan Cove took a little figuring out. There were questions about the right place to go, getting to the boat, and moving the whole group together. But eventually we made it. And when we arrived, the effort felt worth it: water, a pool, beach, music, and a family that was eager to enjoy itself.

Benj was especially excited. In the pool, I carried him on my shoulders, and those moments mattered to me more than I can easily say. We were still getting to know each other. He's a quiet, good kid, and I didn't expect a relationship to happen all at once. But the laughter and play gave us something of our own. I was grateful for every little bit of trust.

There was karaoke, of course. Tito Erick and I sang “Livin' on a Prayer,” while Tita Neth kept returning to “Zombie.” I loved the unapologetic fun of it all. You wore that black dress I still think of when I see our photographs. I remember walking with you along the shore and thinking how beautiful you looked, how caring you were with everyone, and how easily a crowd of relatives could become the background to a moment that belonged only to us.

I watched you and Benj playing in the pool. I watched you taking care of people. It struck me that I wasn't just falling more in love with a woman. I was being allowed to imagine a place in a family. Not an imagined wedding, not a promise that everything would be easy—just the growing feeling that this was where I wanted to belong.

Some of our photos and videos continued after midnight. I still count them as part of this day. A memory doesn't end simply because a clock changes its date.
