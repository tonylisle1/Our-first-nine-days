Day 07 · April 5, 2026

A family at the cove

This resort trip was yours before it was ours. You had planned it for your family before my own visit was even arranged. I loved that about it. I wasn't asking your family to revolve around me; I was fortunate enough to be included in something you already wanted to give them.

We woke to a house full of preparations for the resort outing. There were overnight bags to pack and the groceries we'd bought earlier in the week. This resort outing would also be my first opportunity to meet Tita Bob and Abby. It seemed that every time we counted the passengers there were more people, bags, or food containers to account for. You took photographs of us while we waited for Konsi and his large passenger van. You and I found our place alone on the first bench behind the driver, surrounded by the people you'd brought together. We had about an hour and twenty minutes of road ahead of us.

That ride had one of the most wonderful sounds of the whole trip: little Zia singing “Jingle Bells” with Tita Tinay. She approached the verses almost shyly, then anticipated the chorus and belted it out loudly—sometimes a beat early because she simply couldn't wait. The van filled with laughter. I'd heard her little chipmunk voice in the family home, but here she was putting on a performance for everyone. I am so glad I can still remember it.

Finding the boat was an adventure in itself. The driver had to ask for directions several times before we found the narrow, somewhat questionable road into the embarkation area at Natipuan. We unloaded the van, then carried all our luggage and supplies down a long path toward the boats. I picked up as much as I could so the women wouldn't have to. I was having trouble with bruises and sore spots on my hands already, and you kept noticing them and worrying about me. Your concern meant more to me than the inconvenience of carrying another bag ever could.

We walked up a long wooden plank to board the covered boat and found places among the family. I remember looking back at the shore as we pulled away, then forward at the hills and coves opening before us. The water was astonishingly clear. In places it looked so shallow that I couldn't believe a boat could pass there. This was the Philippines I'd seen in travel pictures, except now it was right under my feet and you were beside me, smiling. The ride took roughly twenty minutes, and every turn seemed to reveal something I had never seen before.

When we arrived at Forest Cove, the boat came in against the sand of a secluded beach. I helped unload and carry everything up the sloping path to our cottage, then turned down another section toward the house. By the time I'd set down the bags and stepped away to take pictures, the children were already swimming. They hadn't even given the place a chance to become familiar first. I admired that. The cottage had its own pool, and the cool water felt wonderful in the heat. Later we tried the sea as well, but the water was surprisingly warm—almost too warm to refresh me.

Benj was especially excited, and the pool gave us room to connect without the distraction of a phone or a video game. He's a quiet boy, and I wasn't expecting instant closeness, but in the water I could help him practice swimming, put him on my shoulders, and laugh with him. Patata, on the other hand, seemed incapable of being afraid of anything. He would throw himself toward the fun whether or not his swimming skills quite matched his enthusiasm. I couldn't help laughing at his fearless energy. Watching you with the children gave me another glimpse of how naturally you cared for them.

After swimming and a meal, you appeared with Nicole, Abby, and Kristen, all dressed up for photographs on the beach. You wore a black dress with a slit along the side, and I remember thinking you looked stunning. For a moment you headed down the steps with the other girls and I wondered whether you were going without me. Then you turned around, came back, and asked if I'd like to join you. Of course I would. I wanted to be beside you whenever I could. I watched the girls take their pictures, and when the others went back up, you and I stayed to walk together by the water and watch the light change. Those are some of my favorite photographs of us, not simply because of how beautiful you looked, but because it felt like a little moment belonging entirely to the two of us.

Later, there was food, the pool, and music. Tito Erick and I sang “Livin' on a Prayer,” and Tita Neth found more than one opportunity to sing “Zombie.” I was delighted by how comfortably everyone sang in front of others. Nobody seemed embarrassed to have fun. But the picture that I return to most often is you playing with Benj in the pool, still wearing your dress. You were laughing with your son. I remember looking at you both and thinking how lucky I was—how glad I was that I'd boarded the plane. I had begun this trip hoping to meet the woman I loved. I was discovering that I wanted a future with her family, too.

I watched you and Benj playing in the pool. I watched you taking care of people. It struck me that I wasn't just falling more in love with a woman. I was being allowed to imagine a place in a family. Not an imagined wedding, not a promise that everything would be easy—just the growing feeling that this was where I wanted to belong.

We wandered by the water later in the night, taking photographs when the clock had already passed midnight. They belong to this same evening in my memory. The beautiful daytime views, the pool, the singing, and that nighttime beach are all chapters of one experience. When we finally rested, I felt happy in the quiet way I had come to recognize around you. I'd found a place where I could imagine being part of a family and a woman whose presence made even an unfamiliar place feel peaceful.
