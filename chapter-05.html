Good Friday, far from home

Good Friday came while I was far from Ohio and the familiar Easter routines of my own family. I spoke with Bella and stayed in touch with my mother. I missed them, of course. At the same time, I was surrounded by your family and discovering that the distance from home didn't make this experience less meaningful. It was asking me to make room in my heart for another home.

There were breakfasts that were new to me, and mangoes so fresh and good that I still remember them. There were outings along the coast near Balayan Bay, a beautiful sunset, and the sense that you were showing me not just a country but a place where real people spent their ordinary days. I remember the water and the light, and I remember the church service during that Easter weekend. None of it felt like a packaged tour.

Back around the family, there were conversations, drinks, laughter, and time together on the porch. One conversation included Reuben and Tito Erick helping bridge the language gap. I appreciated the effort. I was often listening to Tagalog and trying to follow the feeling of what was being said even when I couldn't follow all the words. Being welcomed doesn't always require understanding every sentence.

I remember our walks, too. There was a little shop and then a longer walk where we teased each other about just how far I wanted to go. What stays with me isn't the distance. It is your hand in mine, and that almost instinctive pleasure of walking beside you, looking after you, being the person who could make sure you were comfortable and safe.

Somewhere around this part of our visit I gave you the “Open When” letters I'd prepared, each one wrapped individually with twine. I can't prove the exact day from memory, so I won't pretend I can. But I remember why I made them. I wanted you to have something you could hold and open after I had to leave—words for moments when the miles would feel too large.

When I think about that day, I don't think only about the coast. I think about the closeness of your hand, the sound of family talking, and the odd happiness of being an American far from home who nevertheless felt that he belonged somewhere.
