My Dove, I wanted to give you something more lasting than a collection of vacation photographs. I wanted to remember what it felt like to finally hold you, to learn your world, to become part of your family for a little while, and to discover how much the ordinary moments could mean.

This is my side of our first nine days. Not every minute was photographed. Not every conversation can be reconstructed word for word. But these are the memories I carried home, in the order I lived them, and the feelings I never want to lose.

For you. For us. For the day we can look back together and remember how it all began.

Day 01 · March 30, 2026

The moment the crowd opened

I had spent so much time imagining what that first meeting might feel like. For months, I had known your face and your voice through a screen, and I had already come to associate you with an unusual kind of peace. But there was one enormous question that no amount of messaging could answer: what would it feel like when you were finally standing in front of me?

I barely slept before I left. Bella and Makynna took me to the airport, and then came a day of connections: Cincinnati, Houston, Honolulu, Guam, and finally Manila. Even getting to you had its own little disaster. In Guam, my passport was inside a carry-on bag that had been checked, and for a while I was frightened that I had made it this far only to be stopped before the last flight. Somehow, that was sorted out. All I really wanted was to reach you.

I remember the nighttime air when I emerged in Manila. I remember looking, and looking again. The airport was full of people, but what I was hearing around me barely registered. I was looking for one face. I tried to keep my usual calm exterior, but as the seconds passed and I couldn't find you, the fear started to rise. Had I missed you? Were you somewhere else? Was this really going to happen?

Then the crowd opened, and there you were. Everything I had been worrying about seemed to disappear at once. I remember your smile, your shyness, your sweetness. I remember the small bouquet you had made from pastel ribbons. And above everything else, I remember that first embrace. I had hoped for a feeling of peace when I held you. Instead, I found something so warm and complete that I have struggled to describe it ever since. You later told me I looked relieved. I was. More than you could possibly know.

Tita San took a photograph of us at the airport. It became my WhatsApp background, a reminder of the moment you stopped being a person on my phone and became the woman I could actually hold. There was Benj, too, and the strange, lovely reality that I wasn't just meeting you: I was beginning to meet your world.

On the ride from the airport, Tita San sat up front beside the driver. I was in the back with you and Benj. He started between us, and at some point you moved to the middle. There was small talk about my travel and whether I was tired. I was content to listen. The important part was the simple fact of your hand in mine and your body beside me.

Tita San and Benj went home. We went directly to the hotel together. We checked in, settled into the room, and lay down still dressed in the clothes we had worn all day. There was no grand first-night scene. I am not naturally bold about making a first move, and you were shy, too. We held each other lightly. We looked at each other. I gave you the picture frame with its note and the Graeter's chocolates I had brought from home. I wanted to spend the entire night memorizing your eyes, even as exhaustion finally caught up with me. If our first evening sounds quiet, it was. That is part of why it was perfect.

There are no photographs here to stand in for what I remember. Some moments have to live in words.

Day 02 · March 31, 2026

The first ordinary morning

My first morning waking up beside you was a joy that didn't need any special event to justify it. We said good morning as ordinary couples do. We got ourselves ready and showered separately. These were tiny routines, and I was already discovering how much I loved being included in them.

We went looking for gift bags at a local market. I remember that experience because it wasn't like walking into a familiar American store. There were individual stalls, merchandise everywhere, people bargaining, and a lively, practical kind of shopping that made the place feel like an open-air flea market. Later there was a modern mall, which was a different experience entirely, much closer to what I was used to back home. Even the contrast taught me something about your everyday surroundings.

Somewhere in those Taytay outings came my first ride in one of the motorcycle-and-sidecar tricycles. It was one of those little cultural experiences I could have read about beforehand, but I was glad to experience it while you were right there with me. I wasn't there to be escorted around as a tourist. I was there to see the things that were normal to you.

Then came your home in Taytay and the people who shared it. I won't pretend I wasn't surprised. The living arrangements, the crowded rooms, and the close quarters were very different from the space I knew in Ohio. Some things were hard for me to process. But this was your home and your family, and I wanted to see it honestly, with respect, rather than hide from the contrast. What mattered most was being welcomed into a place that had shaped your life.

I met more of your family, including Mercedes—Tita Bob—and her daughters Abby and Nicole. I gave Benj the LEGO gift I'd carried across the world for him, and we handed out the gifts for your Taytay family. There were chocolates, hometown reminders, sports keychains and other little pieces of where I came from. I had brought things from Ohio, but what I was receiving in return was far more personal: people opening their lives to me. Some of them even gave small keepsakes for Bella and Makynna.

We also shopped for groceries to take to Batangas. That detail might sound unimportant in a list of vacation highlights. But to me it is exactly the kind of detail worth remembering. We were doing errands. We were planning the next day. For a little while, I got to be beside you while life just happened.

Day 03 · April 1, 2026

A place at the family table

We started early—early enough that it was still dark as the family gathered for the journey to Batangas. There were bags to load, people to organize, and all the movement that comes with traveling together as a group. Benj, Tita San, Papa, Nicole, Patata and others were part of that shared adventure. I was watching your family in motion, slowly learning who was who and how everyone fit together.

At the family property, I began to understand a different pace of life. Homes were close to one another. There were hogs being raised nearby. Tito Erick and Tita Neth welcomed us, and I also remember Dennis, Tito Erick's brother, as friendly and cheerful. The room where we first placed our luggage looked almost like a storage area or pantry. Later, we were shown a proper bedroom, and I learned that Tito Erick and Tita Neth had given up their own room for us. I have not forgotten that kindness.

You took charge of my phone and started taking pictures of everything. That still makes me smile. You wanted a record of what I was seeing, and before long I had photographs of experiences I might otherwise have simply carried home in my head. We ate, relaxed with your family, enjoyed coconuts, and explored the landscape around Taal Lake. You showed me the volcano viewpoints, the scenery, and a street-food spot that you loved yourself. I liked that. It wasn't only about showing me attractions. It was you sharing a favorite part of your own world.

I was learning things I had never thought much about before, right down to the differences in bathing facilities and how people arranged their homes. That evening, with portable fans near the bed, I felt especially grateful for the room the family had made for us. The hospitality was real, not ceremonial.

There was also a conversation I carried into this trip with serious intentions. I spoke privately with Tito Erick on the porch and asked for his approval of the possibility of marrying you someday. I wasn't proposing to you. I wasn't announcing a wedding. But I wanted to show respect for the people who loved you, and I wanted them to understand that I did not see you as a passing romance.

Even after a day full of scenery and new people, my favorite part was still the simplest one: lying beside you, holding you, and feeling that strange, familiar peace settle over me again. The sights were beautiful. You were home to me.

Day 04 · April 2, 2026

A promise made quietly

This day felt less like a vacation and more like a glimpse of living together. There wasn't the pressure of getting to the next attraction. We spent time around the house, moved in and out of family conversations, went for groceries, and simply existed in the same space. After so much time spent loving you through a screen, I discovered that being with you while nothing extraordinary was happening could be extraordinary enough.

I remember you going outside for a little sunlight, and the excitement about one of the hogs having babies. I remember you encouraging me to pose for photographs the Filipino way. Some of those poses were outside my comfort zone, but I went along because I loved seeing you enjoy yourself. You had a way of pulling laughter out of me without making me feel foolish.

I had brought something with me that meant more than its size suggested. The promise ring was tucked away in the gift bag connected with your bouquet. I waited until we were alone in our room. I didn't want a performance in front of an audience. I wanted a quiet moment that belonged to us.

I gave you that ring as a promise of what I hoped we were building: something real, lasting, and worth choosing. It wasn't a proposal, and I didn't want it confused with one. But I meant it. I remember your smile and the kiss that followed, and the relief of knowing the tenderness I had been carrying in my heart had somewhere to go.

I also gave you my hoodie. Seeing you wear something of mine—over and over, even when it was far too warm to need it—became one of the memories I love most. A ring could express a promise in a single moment. That hoodie expressed something every time you put it on: the comfort of having a little piece of me close to you.

It is easy to call a day like this uneventful. I don't. We spent it becoming less like two visitors sharing a trip and more like two people discovering the ordinary shape of a life together.

Day 05 · April 3, 2026

Good Friday, far from home

Good Friday came while I was far from Ohio and the familiar Easter routines of my own family. I spoke with Bella and stayed in touch with my mother. I missed them, of course. At the same time, I was surrounded by your family and discovering that the distance from home didn't make this experience less meaningful. It was asking me to make room in my heart for another home.

There were breakfasts that were new to me, and mangoes so fresh and good that I still remember them. There were outings along the coast near Balayan Bay, a beautiful sunset, and the sense that you were showing me not just a country but a place where real people spent their ordinary days. I remember the water and the light, and I remember the church service during that Easter weekend. None of it felt like a packaged tour.

Back around the family, there were conversations, drinks, laughter, and time together on the porch. One conversation included Reuben and Tito Erick helping bridge the language gap. I appreciated the effort. I was often listening to Tagalog and trying to follow the feeling of what was being said even when I couldn't follow all the words. Being welcomed doesn't always require understanding every sentence.

I remember our walks, too. There was a little shop and then a longer walk where we teased each other about just how far I wanted to go. What stays with me isn't the distance. It is your hand in mine, and that almost instinctive pleasure of walking beside you, looking after you, being the person who could make sure you were comfortable and safe.

Somewhere around this part of our visit I gave you the “Open When” letters I'd prepared, each one wrapped individually with twine. I can't prove the exact day from memory, so I won't pretend I can. But I remember why I made them. I wanted you to have something you could hold and open after I had to leave—words for moments when the miles would feel too large.

When I think about that day, I don't think only about the coast. I think about the closeness of your hand, the sound of family talking, and the odd happiness of being an American far from home who nevertheless felt that he belonged somewhere.

Day 06 · April 4, 2026

When the music took over

By now, I wasn't trying quite so hard to figure out how I was supposed to behave. I was becoming comfortable enough to let myself be playful. You had already seen my lopsided half-smile, and you took delight in copying it. You mocked my eye rolls, too, always affectionately. I loved your own expressions—the smile, the eye rolls, those little pursed lips that could melt me without a word.

At one point you ran, and I chased after you. It wasn't some heroic event. What I remember is your surprise that I actually came running after you. It made me laugh, and it reminded me of something I had been discovering all week: looking after you didn't feel like an obligation. It felt natural. I liked being the man who would follow, protect, and be there.

Then there was the celebration, the stage, the music, and the dancing. I found myself joining in front of a crowd with a live band playing. There are experiences I would never have predicted before leaving Ohio, and dancing like that was certainly one of them. The atmosphere was joyous and a little wild. I loved how much fun everyone seemed willing to have, without the self-consciousness I might have expected back home.

You loved the dancing video, and I can understand why you did. It caught a side of me I don't always show. It also caught something I want us to remember: I didn't have to be guarded around you. I could be foolish, laugh, and join in. The moment with the man in the white cap is especially funny now. I hope it always makes you laugh.

I was beginning to imagine more than another visit. Seeing daily life, the countryside, your family, and the way you moved through all of it made me wonder what having a place in the Philippines might look like. I wasn't making a final plan. I was noticing that the idea of spending more time here no longer seemed far-fetched. You were changing the way I imagined the future.

And when the noise finally faded, there you still were: the person whose company I wanted most whether I was dancing with strangers or sitting in the quiet.

Day 07 · April 5, 2026

A family at the cove

This resort trip was yours before it was ours. You had planned it for your family before my own visit was even arranged. I loved that about it. I wasn't asking your family to revolve around me; I was fortunate enough to be included in something you already wanted to give them.

We packed up and waited for the big passenger van. You took pictures of us while we waited. Once everyone was aboard, you and I sat together on the first bench behind the front seats. The drive became its own little story, especially when little Zia started singing “Jingle Bells” with Tita Tinay. She would sing the verses quietly, almost cautiously, but when the chorus came she couldn't contain herself. She belted it out, sometimes a beat early, and everyone laughed. I can still picture the excitement.

Reaching Forest Cove Beach House at Natipuan Cove took a little figuring out. There were questions about the right place to go, getting to the boat, and moving the whole group together. But eventually we made it. And when we arrived, the effort felt worth it: water, a pool, beach, music, and a family that was eager to enjoy itself.

Benj was especially excited. In the pool, I carried him on my shoulders, and those moments mattered to me more than I can easily say. We were still getting to know each other. He's a quiet, good kid, and I didn't expect a relationship to happen all at once. But the laughter and play gave us something of our own. I was grateful for every little bit of trust.

There was karaoke, of course. Tito Erick and I sang “Livin' on a Prayer,” while Tita Neth kept returning to “Zombie.” I loved the unapologetic fun of it all. You wore that black dress I still think of when I see our photographs. I remember walking with you along the shore and thinking how beautiful you looked, how caring you were with everyone, and how easily a crowd of relatives could become the background to a moment that belonged only to us.

I watched you and Benj playing in the pool. I watched you taking care of people. It struck me that I wasn't just falling more in love with a woman. I was being allowed to imagine a place in a family. Not an imagined wedding, not a promise that everything would be easy—just the growing feeling that this was where I wanted to belong.

Some of our photos and videos continued after midnight. I still count them as part of this day. A memory doesn't end simply because a clock changes its date.

Day 08 · April 6, 2026

“Family picture”

We got up early enough to enjoy the beach before we had to leave Forest Cove. A small group went down to the sand: you, me, Benj, Tita San, Tita Tinay, Tita Neth, Patata, Chuckoy, and others. There were the usual attempts to arrange people for photographs, and then something happened that I don't think anyone realized would stay with me so strongly.

Tita Neth suggested a “Family picture.” She meant one of just you, Benj, and me. Tita Tinay was likely the person taking it. To everyone else, it might have been a simple instruction about who should stand where. To me, it meant the world. The three of us together, named as a family. I didn't need a grand speech. Two ordinary words made me feel included in a future I had hardly dared to picture.

The kids wanted more time in the pool. There was breakfast, swimming, and a banana-boat ride. We had to check out, but I wasn't ready for that part of the day to end. I even tried to extend the stay. Forest Cove was already booked, so there wasn't room for us to remain. I remember how much I wanted the children to have just a little more fun, even if that meant paying for time I wouldn't be there to enjoy.

On the drive back, you fell asleep against me. That is another of those moments I treasure. No camera trick could improve it. You were simply tired, comfortable enough to rest on me, and I got to hold you while the road carried us back.

Instead of letting the outing end, we found RMS Garden Resort in Lemery. There were pools and slides, and we brought pizza. Once again there was the excited energy of family making the most of the time together. For me, the best moments were not necessarily the loudest ones. I remember being alone with you in the water. I remember the kisses. I remember wishing that these could be ordinary evenings instead of rare ones.

By now the idea of going home was getting harder to think about. It wasn't that I'd discovered a perfect vacation. I had discovered how much I wanted the unremarkable days with you, too—the days no one thinks to photograph.

Day 09 · April 7, 2026

The goodbye I never wanted

We woke around five in the morning. I suggested that we brush our teeth and get cleaned up, and then we went outside for a little quiet time together. There was a sitting area near the beach with large cube-shaped cushions. We settled in and talked until we were told we had to move because the spot was intended for photographs. We laughed about that and went looking for coffee. I wish I could have made those ordinary minutes last much longer.

There was Jollibee for breakfast, more swimming, and then the practical reality of packing and checking out. We went back to the family home. I had a bag to organize and a flight to catch, and neither task interested me very much. Work was waiting in America. I had thought about extending my stay, but the obligations I'd left behind would not allow it.

Before leaving, I gave you the necklace and earrings I had brought for your approaching birthday and our three-month anniversary. You wanted to open the gift right away, so of course I let you. The gifts mattered because they were for you; what mattered more was being able to watch your face as you received them. There were farewells to your family. Tita Neth gave me a small Tagalog pocket Bible. I gave Chuckoy my Ohio State beanie. Each goodbye made the departure a little more real.

On the way toward Manila, you held my arm and sometimes slept against me. I quietly took photographs of you, trying to save even these last moments. I didn't want to wake you. I wanted to remember the weight of your head and the knowledge that for a little longer, at least, we were still together.

At the airport I made a mistake that I still regret. I passed through a checkpoint thinking I would be able to step back outside and say goodbye properly. I couldn't. Once I realized that, the last embrace I had counted on was already out of reach. We had to say goodbye separated by a barrier. After nine days of finally being able to hold you, that was a terribly hard way to leave.

I gave Benj my scarlet-and-gray Ohio State cap and asked him to look after his mother. Then there was the flight, the distance, and tears I tried not to show. I had arrived in Manila afraid I might not find you in the crowd. I left wishing more than anything that I could get back through the crowd to you one last time.

I didn't leave wondering whether what we had was real. I left knowing. That certainty made the goodbye hurt, but it also gave me something to carry home: the knowledge that these first nine days were not the whole story. They were only the beginning.

What I carried home

I remember you calling me fluffy when we cuddled, like a teddy bear. Sometimes you said I reminded you of a baby, and I knew you meant it with affection. I remember how you copied my crooked smile and my eye rolls. I remember the way you pursed your lips and the way you looked at me when you thought I was being silly.

I remember tracing the skin on your arm or brushing back your hair, caught up in the strange need to memorize the person I had waited so long to touch. I remember your concern when you noticed the bruises and little sores on my hands, as though they worried you more than they worried me.

I remember how naturally you reached for my hand in public, how you wrapped yourself around my arm, and how peaceful it felt. I remember Benj and the people who welcomed me. I remember laughter around the table, a little shop, a sleepy ride, and someone calling the three of us together for a family picture.

I remember the crowded airport when I arrived and the barrier between us when I left. Between those two moments, nine days gave me a lifetime of memories.

They weren't enough. They could never have been enough. But they were ours, and they changed what the word home could mean to me.
