For my little Dove

Before there was a memory, there was a moment.

I thought I was flying across the world to finally meet the woman I loved. I didn't know that in just nine days, I'd discover a thousand little things I never wanted to live without.

These are the days the way I remember them. Just us—and everything you made me feel.

With all my love,
Tony

A first-person memoir · Working edition

Nine days. One beginning.

DAY 01 · March 30, 2026

The Moment You Were Real

I barely slept before the trip. That happens to me before early flights, but this was no ordinary trip. Bella and Makynna took me to the airport, and I left knowing I was taking perhaps the biggest calculated risk of my life. I had spent months getting to know you across an ocean. What if I got all the way to Manila and nobody was there? I kept that question tucked away, even while I looked calm on the outside.

Four flights stood between Ohio and you: Cincinnati to Houston, then Honolulu, Guam, and finally Manila. In Houston, my carry-on was checked because the overhead bins were full. I had forgotten my passport was inside it. I didn't realize the problem until Guam, where they announced that everyone should have a passport ready to board. Suddenly I wasn't so calm. Airport workers helped track down the bag, and I rushed through customs and security, hoping I wouldn't miss the last flight. I can laugh at it now. I certainly wasn't laughing then.

By the time I reached Manila, I had barely slept. I remember walking out and searching for you, almost as if the rest of the airport had disappeared. And then I saw you coming toward me through the crowd. There was your smile, your eyes, and that sweet, soft voice I had grown to love. You hugged me tightly. Whatever uncertainty I had carried across the Pacific seemed to melt away in that one embrace.

You had brought me a little bouquet made from pastel-colored ribbon. Benj was shy and quiet, and Tita San was there to welcome me too. I insisted on carrying my own luggage. In the car, with Benj between us at first, we held hands. Then you moved beside me, and I put my arm around you. Outside the windows, the traffic was an extraordinary kind of organized chaos. Inside that car, all I wanted was to keep looking at you.

Tita San and Benj went home. You and I went to the hotel, checked in, and lay down together still wearing our clothes. We were both a little shy. There was no dramatic first kiss, no grand scene. We simply held each other and looked into each other's eyes. I gave you the picture frame with the note inside—the frame you could fill with your favorite photograph of us—and some Graeter's chocolates from home. I remember how much the letter meant to you.

I wanted to stay awake and look at you all night. My body, exhausted from the flights, had other ideas. But I knew something important before I finally slept. The peace I had felt with you from the beginning hadn't been imaginary. It was real. And you were right there in my arms.

DAY 02 · March 31, 2026

Your World, and a Place for Me

I woke up beside you. Even now, that simple sentence is enough to bring me back. It was something I had wanted for so long: an ordinary morning with the person I loved. We exchanged the kinds of small good mornings that people hardly remember. I remember how good it felt that we could finally say them without a screen between us.

We went to the market to find gift bags for everything I'd brought. It wasn't a modern American mall. It felt more like an open-air flea market in the middle of town, little privately owned stalls and booths with people buying, selling, and bargaining. Later, at the actual mall, I recognized something much more familiar. The two places couldn't have been more different. And everywhere we went, I felt conspicuously tall and unmistakably foreign, but I mostly encountered curiosity and kindness.

Then I saw your home in Taytay and met the people who lived there. The cramped rooms, crowded belongings, and sleeping arrangements were a culture shock for me. I had never experienced anything quite like it. It made me think about how much comfort I take for granted back home. But what mattered most was how warmly I was welcomed. These were your people, and you were letting me into your everyday life.

I gave Benj his LEGO gift and shared the gifts I'd brought for the Taytay household. We went shopping for groceries to bring to Batangas. I met more of your family, including Ta Bob and her daughters Abby and Nicole. There was so much to absorb: the faces, the names, the rhythm of a home where so much family life happened together.

I didn't fly halfway around the world to see malls or tourist attractions. I came for you. Yet already, on that second day, the Philippines was becoming more than a place on the map. It was becoming part of our story.

DAY 03 · April 1, 2026

The Road to Batangas

We got up while it was still dark and went to your house, where we gathered everyone for the drive to Batangas. I hadn't expected to meet your father out front that morning. We shook hands, and with his limited English and my nonexistent Tagalog, we didn't exchange many words. Sometimes a handshake has to do the talking.

The trip introduced me to the homes, land, and people who had been part of your life long before I arrived. Tito Erick and Tita Neth welcomed us. I met Dennis, who always seemed ready with a smile, and saw the family property where homes stood close together and hogs were raised. I wasn't watching a travel documentary anymore. I was standing in the middle of the life you knew.

Our bags first went into what looked to me like a storage room. Later, you moved them to a bedroom. It took me a while to realize that the family had given up Tito Erick and Tita Neth's own room and bed for us. That was a kind of hospitality I won't forget.

We visited, rested, ate, and visited some more. You started taking charge of the photographs with my phone, because you knew I wasn't much of a photographer. I loved that you did. We went out with Tito Erick and Tita Neth in the trike, saw places overlooking Taal Lake, and visited an area where you enjoyed the street food. Looking back, it meant a lot that you included somewhere you loved while still showing me the things you thought I'd want to see.

There were so many moments that made me smile—new foods, new sights, even learning how to manage a traditional bucket-and-tabo bath. But my favorite part came when we were back in our room. It was hot. The fans moved the air around us, and there was family life going on outside our door. I didn't care. I was holding you. I discovered, almost to my surprise, just how much I loved to cuddle. And I never wanted to let you go.

DAY 04 · April 2, 2026

A Promise in an Ordinary Day

One of my favorite things about our trip was that not every day needed to be an adventure. This morning felt almost like a day off work at home, except I was with you. We went outside for some sunlight, and there was news that one of the hogs had given birth. You tried to get me into those traditional Filipino picture poses that always made me laugh. I'm not a man who poses for pictures, but somehow you could talk me into it.

We went grocery shopping again. I remember being with you and the kids, buying snacks while Tita Tinay handled much of the shopping. These were ordinary family errands, and I liked being part of them. Even the unfamiliar sights and smells in the store became another detail of the world I was learning through you.

Later, we were alone in the bedroom, relaxing together. I hadn't planned an elaborate moment. I had brought a ring because I wanted you to know what my commitment meant. I had hidden it in the gift bag from the bouquet you gave me. I remember taking it out and telling you, in my own imperfect words, that it was a symbol that I was yours, that I promised to be yours, and hoped you'd be mine. It wasn't an engagement ring. It was a promise that I was serious about us.

You smiled and kissed me. We cuddled. I gave you my hoodie, too. You loved that hoodie and wore it again and again, sometimes even when it was too warm to make any sense. Later, you told me you missed its smell. I loved the thought that a little piece of me could stay close to you even after I went home.

The ring made something visible that I already felt. I wasn't suddenly choosing you for the first time. I had been choosing you. But that afternoon I got to put a symbol of it in your hands.

DAY 05 · April 3, 2026

Mangoes, Sunsets, and Small Traditions

Being away from my family during Easter weekend was not an easy decision. Yet being with you and your family felt right. Morning meals were a lesson all their own. Fish, rice, vegetables, clams—there didn't seem to be a sharp line between breakfast and any other meal. And then there were the fresh mangoes. Oh my goodness. I had never tasted mango like that at home.

Somewhere around this part of the trip, I believe I gave you the Open When letters. I cannot honestly swear to the exact day, and I don't want to pretend I can. I do remember preparing them so carefully back in Ohio: each letter in its own envelope, each one individually tied with twine like a tiny present. I wanted there to be something you could physically open on the days when I couldn't be there beside you.

That evening we went out again. Tito Erick and Tita Neth came along, and your father joined us too. We visited the rocky coast near Banoy as the tide drew out, with a sunset that made the place feel even more beautiful. Later we went to a church service. I remember not just the places, but the feeling of being invited into the traditions and habits of your family.

And of course I remember you. I remember the way you looked, the way you laughed, the way our little walks and handholding were becoming so natural. I always enjoyed the sights, but what made the trip extraordinary was that every view came with you standing beside me.

DAY 06 · April 4, 2026

The Night I Danced

We had a slow start after the night before. You were feeling a little rough, and I was happy simply to rest beside you. I never felt that an uneventful morning was wasted. Being together was the point. We took a walk and spent time enjoying the ordinary rhythm around the house.

I remember how you would tease me. My lopsided half-smile became a source of amusement for you. So did my eye rolls. You didn't just copy them; you playfully mocked me with those expressions of yours, and I loved it. You had a gift for making me laugh without even trying.

There was a lively celebration nearby with a stage and a band. At some point I found myself dancing in front of a crowd. You loved that moment so much that the video became one of your favorites. I was a stranger in a new country, and yet I could be myself in the middle of all those people. I loved the friendliness and warmth I felt around me.

I remember another playful moment when you ran and I chased after you. What mattered to me wasn't the running itself. It was the look of surprise that I'd chased you, and how quickly the moment turned into laughter and affection. I found myself thinking about how natural it felt to look after you.

I'd gone to the Philippines for you and your family, not to shop for a new life somewhere else. But by this point I was falling in love with the country too. I even caught myself imagining what it would be like to live there. Still, once the music and people were behind us, nothing could compete with being able to hold you again.

DAY 07 · April 5, 2026

A New Kind of Family

We packed for the family resort trip you'd planned well before I ever booked my flight. A large van came for all of us, and the trip felt like an adventure before we even reached the water. You and I sat together in the front bench row, while everybody else filled the van with conversation, laughter, and excitement.

One of my favorite memories was little Zia—our chipmunk—singing Jingle Bells with Tita Tinay. She was quiet through the verses, but as soon as she knew the chorus was coming she'd belt it out, sometimes a beat too early because she simply couldn't wait. Everybody laughed. I can still hear it.

We reached Forest Cove Beach House at Natipuan Cove after a confusing search for where to catch the boat and how to get there. Soon we were unpacked and enjoying the water. Benj was so excited. In the pool, away from his phone and games, we finally got to play together in a way we hadn't before. I remember him on my shoulders. It was one of the strongest moments of bonding I had with him.

That night was full of swimming, singing, and family fun. Tito Erick and I joined in on Livin' on a Prayer. I remember the beach and the water and, especially, you in that black dress. We walked together, and you were so beautiful I hardly knew what to do with the feeling.

There's a picture in my mind of you and Benj playing together in the pool, you still in that dress. I remember thinking, How lucky am I? The greatest part of the day was not the resort or the entertainment. It was realizing I wasn't only imagining a future with you. I was beginning to feel like I had a family with you.

DAY 08 · April 6, 2026

Family Picture

We woke early enough to enjoy the beach before checkout. A group of us went down to the sand for photographs: you, me, Benj, Tita San, Tita Tinay, Tita Neth, Patata, and Chuckoy. There is one moment I never want to lose. Tita Neth called out, 'Family picture,' meaning a picture of just you, Benj, and me. Other people agreed, and Tita Tinay was there taking pictures. That little phrase meant more to me than anyone could have known.

We went back to the cottage for breakfast and more swimming. The boys wanted the pool again. We took a banana-boat ride, traveling outside the cove and stopping to swim before returning. Everyone seemed to be having so much fun, and I didn't want it to end.

Before leaving, I asked whether we could stay another night at Forest Cove. You called Monica, the owner, but the place was already booked. On the return journey I held you as you slept against my shoulder. Back at the house, I couldn't get the idea of one more resort night out of my head. Tito Erick told us about some nearby options, and we went to look.

We found RMS Garden Resort in Lemery. It had pools, slides, cabanas, and a wonderful suite looking out toward the beach. I wanted the children to have another day of swimming, even if I couldn't stay with them for all of it. We arranged the room, collected everyone, and brought pizza back for the group.

That final evening, the happiest moments were the simplest ones: being with everyone, swimming with just you, and having a little time alone together. Underneath it all I was beginning to realize how desperately I wanted time to stop. I didn't want to visit you only a couple of times a year. I wanted the ordinary days, every day.

DAY 09 · April 7, 2026

Until I Could Hold You Again

We woke around five and went to brush our teeth and get ready. There was an outdoor seating area between our suite and the beach, where we sat close together for a while. We eventually found coffee, including some for your papa, and ordered Jollibee for breakfast. Chicken and spaghetti in the morning still made me smile. We swam again before it was finally time to check out.

Back at the house, I had to face the thing I had been trying not to think about: packing my suitcase. I gave you the necklace and earrings I had brought for your upcoming birthday and our three-month anniversary. I had hoped you would wait to open them, but I wanted to see your face too. Of course you asked if you could open them. I gave in.

I said goodbye to everyone I could. Tita Neth gave me a little Tagalog Bible, which touched me deeply after all our conversations about faith. I gave Chuckoy my Ohio State beanie. Then I learned that Benj and the boys, along with Tita San and Tita Tinay, would be coming to Manila to see me off. I loved that.

In Konsi's vehicle, you held onto my arm and sometimes fell asleep. I took pictures of you like that, quietly. Those pictures mean so much because they remind me exactly how your hand felt holding on. I watched you whether you were awake or asleep. I had looked into extending my flight by a day, and I later learned you had wanted to ask me to stay. Work and the end of my vacation time made that impossible.

At the airport, I made a mistake. I entered the area for baggage check believing I could come back out and say goodbye properly. After waiting in line, I discovered that we couldn't meet the way I had imagined. I found you on the other side of a barrier, but it kept us from the embrace I wanted. It was my greatest regret of the trip. I gave Benj the scarlet-and-gray Ohio State cap I'd intended for him all along, and asked him to take care of his mother.

I had crossed an ocean wondering whether you were real. I left knowing exactly what I wanted. The trip had ended, but my desire to be with you had only grown stronger. And before long, from San Francisco, there was your face again on my screen. It wasn't the same as holding you. But it meant we had already begun finding our way back to each other.

The little things

The memories between the memories.

How you called me fluffy, like a teddy bear. How you teased that half-smile and rolled your eyes back at my eye rolls. The way you held my hand, held onto my arm, and made even the quietest moments feel like the ones I never wanted to end.

I discovered something on that trip: sometimes all I want is to hold you and have nowhere else in the world I need to be.
