The first ordinary morning

My first morning waking up beside you was a joy that didn't need any special event to justify it. We said good morning as ordinary couples do. We got ourselves ready and showered separately. These were tiny routines, and I was already discovering how much I loved being included in them.

We went looking for gift bags at a local market. I remember that experience because it wasn't like walking into a familiar American store. There were individual stalls, merchandise everywhere, people bargaining, and a lively, practical kind of shopping that made the place feel like an open-air flea market. Later there was a modern mall, which was a different experience entirely, much closer to what I was used to back home. Even the contrast taught me something about your everyday surroundings.

Somewhere in those Taytay outings came my first ride in one of the motorcycle-and-sidecar tricycles. It was one of those little cultural experiences I could have read about beforehand, but I was glad to experience it while you were right there with me. I wasn't there to be escorted around as a tourist. I was there to see the things that were normal to you.

Then came your home in Taytay and the people who shared it. I won't pretend I wasn't surprised. The living arrangements, the crowded rooms, and the close quarters were very different from the space I knew in Ohio. Some things were hard for me to process. But this was your home and your family, and I wanted to see it honestly, with respect, rather than hide from the contrast. What mattered most was being welcomed into a place that had shaped your life.

I met more of your family, including Mercedes—Tita Bob—and her daughters Abby and Nicole. I gave Benj the LEGO gift I'd carried across the world for him, and we handed out the gifts for your Taytay family. There were chocolates, hometown reminders, sports keychains and other little pieces of where I came from. I had brought things from Ohio, but what I was receiving in return was far more personal: people opening their lives to me. Some of them even gave small keepsakes for Bella and Makynna.

We also shopped for groceries to take to Batangas. That detail might sound unimportant in a list of vacation highlights. But to me it is exactly the kind of detail worth remembering. We were doing errands. We were planning the next day. For a little while, I got to be beside you while life just happened.
