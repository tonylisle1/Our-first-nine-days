Day 08 · April 6, 2026

“Family picture”

We got up early enough to enjoy the beach before we had to leave Forest Cove. A small group went down to the sand: you, me, Benj, Tita San, Tita Tinay, Tita Neth, Patata, Chuckoy, and others. There were the usual attempts to arrange people for photographs, and then something happened that I don't think anyone realized would stay with me so strongly.

We walked down to the beach that morning while the light was still fresh. The photographs show the sand, the clear water, and the family making the most of the last hours at Forest Cove. Tita Neth's words—“Family picture”—are so important to me because she was talking about you, Benj, and me. That picture of the three of us would mean more than words to me. To me, it was a glimpse of the family I hoped we could become. I want this moment preserved beside the pictures, not simply mentioned in passing.

Tita Neth suggested a “Family picture.” She meant one of just you, Benj, and me. I believe Tita Tinay was taking the photograph, although I can't swear to that detail now. To everyone else, it might have been a simple instruction about who should stand where. To me, it meant the world. The three of us together, named as a family. I didn't need a grand speech. Two ordinary words made me feel included in a future I had hardly dared to picture.

The kids wanted more time in the pool. There was breakfast, swimming, and a banana-boat ride. We had to check out, but I wasn't ready for that part of the day to end. I even tried to extend the stay. Forest Cove was already booked, so there wasn't room for us to remain. I remember how much I wanted the children to have just a little more fun, even if that meant paying for time I wouldn't be there to enjoy.

Later, as everyone gathered to head home, we made time for another group photograph under the trees. Seeing so many familiar faces together in one picture reminds me how much more this trip became than a visit between just two people. And there is the photograph of you, Benj, and me together on the ride back—a little record of the three of us in the same frame. Those are the images I want to come back to years from now.

On the drive back, you fell asleep against me. That is another of those moments I treasure. No camera trick could improve it. You were simply tired, comfortable enough to rest on me, and I got to hold you while the road carried us back.

Coming home from Forest Cove should have meant that our outing was finished. Everyone had already enjoyed the water, the boat, the food, and one another. But I couldn't stop thinking about how much fun the children were having. We had tried to extend the stay at Forest Cove, only to learn that Monica had another booking. Back at the family house, Tita Neth was already singing again, and I brought up the possibility of finding somewhere else nearby. Tito Erick knew a couple of places. Before long, you, he, and I went out to look.

We visited RMS Garden Resort in Lemery. I remember being shown around rather than simply handed a room key. There was a pool for little ones, a larger adult pool, a children's pool with water features, and two big slides that began from the same raised platform and ended in different pools. Little cabanas lined the pool area; people were gathering, eating, and enjoying their own music. It was exactly the kind of place where the family could squeeze a little more happiness out of the trip.

Then we toured the suites. Each one seemed different, and the last was the biggest and best. I remember the arrangement: a common area and sink, sleeping rooms, bathrooms, a dining area, and an upper room with a balcony overlooking the water. You and I were given that room upstairs. The cost was surprisingly reasonable to me, especially because swimming access came with it. I paid for it gladly. I was going to leave the next day, and there was nothing I wanted more than to make this last stretch of our time together something everyone would remember.

On our way back to the house, we ordered a stack of pizzas. Then came the logistics of getting everybody to the resort in a small number of tricycle trips. I still find it amazing how many people and bags could be made to fit in those things. The kids went ahead so they could start swimming, and the rest of us followed with the pizza. I loved that the whole complicated plan somehow came together.

The pools were full of family laughter. Patata seemed to have limitless energy, dashing between whatever attraction caught his eye. You and I eventually slipped into the adult pool beside the children's area, where we could look at each other and have a quieter moment. I stood comfortably where the water was deep enough that you often couldn't touch the bottom. Some girls nearby apparently made a teasing remark about me; you leaned in and told me they thought I was “yummy.” We laughed about your mock jealousy. The only person I had any interest in was the tiny woman standing right in front of me.

At one point you needed help climbing out. I lifted you onto the edge of the pool, and the little gasp you gave—“GOODNESS!”—still makes me grin. You had seen me as the man who helped with chores and carried bags, but that effortless little lift surprised you. It was only a second, the sort of moment no camera would necessarily catch, and that's exactly why I want it written here.

That evening everyone ate pizza, and the singing started again. Tita Neth returned to “Zombie,” which by then felt like the soundtrack of our visit. I stayed downstairs awhile with the men. Then I realized you had gone upstairs and weren't coming back down. I loved spending time with them, but I could feel the evening slipping away. I excused myself, explaining that this was my last night and I needed to be with you. When I came upstairs and told you why, you were surprised and touched. I hadn't traveled across the ocean to spend my last night apart from you. We held each other for the rest of it. Somewhere in the night there was a short walk beside the dark sand and the sea. Mostly, though, I remember wanting that night to last.
