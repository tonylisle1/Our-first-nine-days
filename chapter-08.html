Day 08 · April 6, 2026

“Family picture”

We got up early enough to enjoy the beach before we had to leave Forest Cove. A small group went down to the sand: you, me, Benj, Tita San, Tita Tinay, Tita Neth, Patata, Chuckoy, and others. There were the usual attempts to arrange people for photographs, and then something happened that I don't think anyone realized would stay with me so strongly.

Tita Neth suggested a “Family picture.” She meant one of just you, Benj, and me. Tita Tinay was likely the person taking it. To everyone else, it might have been a simple instruction about who should stand where. To me, it meant the world. The three of us together, named as a family. I didn't need a grand speech. Two ordinary words made me feel included in a future I had hardly dared to picture.

The kids wanted more time in the pool. There was breakfast, swimming, and a banana-boat ride. We had to check out, but I wasn't ready for that part of the day to end. I even tried to extend the stay. Forest Cove was already booked, so there wasn't room for us to remain. I remember how much I wanted the children to have just a little more fun, even if that meant paying for time I wouldn't be there to enjoy.

On the drive back, you fell asleep against me. That is another of those moments I treasure. No camera trick could improve it. You were simply tired, comfortable enough to rest on me, and I got to hold you while the road carried us back.

Instead of letting the outing end, we found RMS Garden Resort in Lemery. There were pools and slides, and we brought pizza. Once again there was the excited energy of family making the most of the time together. For me, the best moments were not necessarily the loudest ones. I remember being alone with you in the water. I remember the kisses. I remember wishing that these could be ordinary evenings instead of rare ones.

By now the idea of going home was getting harder to think about. It wasn't that I'd discovered a perfect vacation. I had discovered how much I wanted the unremarkable days with you, too—the days no one thinks to photograph.
