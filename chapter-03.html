Day 03 · April 1, 2026

A place at the family table

We checked out of the hotel while it was still dark. It was hard to leave the place where we'd spent our first nights together, but we had a whole new part of the journey ahead of us. We loaded my luggage into a rideshare car and drove to your home in Taytay, where everyone was gathering for our trip to Batangas.

When we arrived, two people were waiting outside whom I had never met in person: your father, whom I would come to call Papa, and your younger brother, Patata. I'd already met other members of your family, but this was my first chance to meet them. I remember stepping into that early-morning scene, making those introductions, and realizing that the circle of people welcoming me was growing. It meant something to me to finally meet two more people who were so important in your life.

We weren't leaving for Batangas just yet. The van still hadn't arrived. While we waited, the family gathered the luggage, gifts, groceries, and everything we'd need for the journey. With so many people and bags, I wondered how we would all fit. When the van finally came, everyone found a place and we loaded up. I was watching your family in motion, slowly learning who was who and how everyone fit together.

Only then did the drive begin. It was still early, and the crowded vehicle carried all of us toward a part of your world I had never seen. I remember the half-awake feeling of that morning, the family around us, and the excitement of going somewhere new with you beside me. The photographs from inside the van bring that moment back: not our arrival at your home, but the journey that came after those first introductions.

I still smile when I think about Tita Neth's first reaction to me. I had expected to be the nervous outsider, but she was the one giggling and hiding, reluctant to step out and greet me. That little exchange made an impression because it was so human. The surroundings were unfamiliar, but shyness, laughter, kindness, and curiosity needed no translation. Soon I was meeting everyone, hearing little Zia's distinctive voice, and trying to keep the names and relationships straight. Later, from bed, her chirping laughter drifted through the spaces above the walls. We began to call her our little chipmunk. That sound became one of the things I remember most fondly about the house.

I also remember how determined everyone seemed to be to feed me. There was fresh coconut from outside and home-cooked food, and then more food, and then seemingly another meal before I'd had time to be hungry again. We would joke about the hobbits' first breakfast and second breakfast, and the joke never seemed far from the truth. I was amazed by the hospitality in a home where everyone seemed to be cooking, eating, chatting or doing something useful.

At the family property, I began to understand a different pace of life. Homes were close to one another. There were hogs being raised nearby. Tito Erick and Tita Neth welcomed us, and I also remember Dennis, Tito Erick's brother, as friendly and cheerful. The room where we first placed our luggage looked almost like a storage area or pantry. Later, we were shown a proper bedroom, and I learned that Tito Erick and Tita Neth had given up their own room for us. I have not forgotten that kindness.

You took charge of my phone and started taking pictures of everything. That still makes me smile. You wanted a record of what I was seeing, and before long I had photographs of experiences I might otherwise have simply carried home in my head. We ate, relaxed with your family, enjoyed coconuts, and explored the landscape around Taal Lake. You showed me the volcano viewpoints, the scenery, and a street-food spot that you loved yourself. I liked that. It wasn't only about showing me attractions. It was you sharing a favorite part of your own world.

Later, when we reached the lookout, I saw a volcano for the first time in my life. I had seen them in pictures and on television, but standing beside you with the real landscape spread before us was something else. We took photographs together, and as daylight faded the moon seemed to sit directly above the peak of a little mountain. I especially love the pictures in which we're both there. The scenery was extraordinary, but the person beside me is what made it unforgettable.

Before I ever arrived, I had told you over WhatsApp that I had never seen a volcano. You remembered. When you took me to Taal Lake, I understood that this was not just a sightseeing stop you had picked at random. You had heard me say something I wanted to experience and found a way to share it with me. The volcano stood alone in the middle of the lake. I had never seen anything like it. Tito Erick and Tita Neth came along as we visited different sights around the water. I learned only afterward that this was, in its own way, their very first date. Getting to treat all four of us to that evening meant a lot to me. I had traveled to get to know you, but that day I got to see you remembering the little things I had said—and I got to share a beautiful place with people who were already making me feel welcome.

That evening we stopped at a place with food, drinks and a view across the water. The lights and music were lively, and I enjoyed sitting with the family after the long day. Later, back at the house, we drank Red Horse on the porch. Tito Erick poured the beer over ice in a pitcher, which he called the Filipino way. I could laugh about watering down the beer, but I was grateful to be included. You were there earlier in the evening, too. Eventually you went inside, leaving Tito Erick and me together. That's when I asked him privately what he thought about me someday asking you to marry me. I was not proposing that night. I wanted to tell him how serious my intentions were. He seemed to understand, and his acceptance meant a great deal to me.

The night didn't end only with that happy conversation. Later you became ill and had to get up repeatedly. I tried to help you into the bathroom, and when you wanted privacy I respected it. I made sure you had a place to rest when you came back and stayed close enough to help when you needed me. I remember the sweetness of being able to care for you in a small way. It was one of the first times I felt that loving you wasn't only about the wonderful moments in the photographs. It was about the hard, unglamorous hours too.

When we finally settled in for the night, I was still absorbing how differently daily life worked here—from the bathing arrangements to the way relatives shared space and made room for guests. Portable fans helped make the bedroom comfortable. I felt especially grateful that Tito Erick and Tita Neth had given us their room. Their hospitality wasn't ceremonial; it was a kindness I could feel in the little practical things.

Even after a day full of scenery and new people, my favorite part was still the simplest one: lying beside you, holding you, and feeling that strange, familiar peace settle over me again. The sights were beautiful. You were home to me.
